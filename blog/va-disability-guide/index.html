VA Disability Guide: Understanding Compensation and Claims

July 14, 2026 | 10 min read | By Military Life Tools

VA disability compensation is one of the most important benefits for veterans who have service-connected conditions. Because the claims process can be complicated and the rating system is detailed, many veterans search for simple explanations before they file or review a decision.

What people usually want to understand

  • How VA disability ratings work
  • What a service connection means
  • How a disability estimate may help with planning
  • What to review before filing or appealing

Why this topic matters

Many veterans are looking for a practical starting point, not a legal lecture. A clear guide can help them understand the basics and decide whether they need deeper help or a calculator-based estimate.

How the rating system works

The VA assigns a percentage rating to each condition based on how it affects daily functioning. Ratings are not added directly: 50% and 30% combine to about 65%, not 80%. The combined rating is then rounded to the nearest 10 and determines your monthly pay rate. Without a calculator, it is easy to underestimate or overestimate your compensation.

What evidence you need

Strong documentation is the difference between a denied claim and a successful one. The VA looks at service records, medical records, private physician statements, and a clear explanation of how the condition affects work, sleep, relationships, and daily functioning. Bottom line

VA disability planning is easier when you have a simple starting point. A calculator and a clear guide can help veterans make sense of the process without getting overwhelmed.

Frequently asked questions

What is VA disability?

VA disability is a tax-free monthly compensation paid to veterans with service-connected medical conditions. The rating determines the amount paid each month, and dependents can increase the rate.

<

How do I file a VA disability claim?

You can file a VA disability claim online at VA.gov, by mail using VA Form 21-526EZ, in person at a VA regional office, or with help from a Veterans Service Organization (VSO). Many veterans benefit from a VSO representative because they help gather evidence and complete forms correctly.

<

What is service-connected disability?

A service-connected disability is a condition that was caused by, aggravated by, or occurred during active military service. The link between service and the condition is the key factor in getting a VA rating.

<

What is a VA disability rating?

A VA disability rating is the percentage (0% to 100%) the VA assigns to each condition. The rating reflects how much the condition affects your ability to function and is the basis for monthly compensation.

<

How is VA disability pay calculated?

The VA combines individual ratings using a formula, then rounds to the nearest 10. The combined rating determines the monthly pay rate, which depends on whether you have dependents. Use a VA disability calculator to estimate your combined rating.

<

Can I appeal a VA decision?

Yes. If you disagree with a VA decision, you can request a Higher-Level Review, file a Supplemental Claim with new evidence, or appeal to the Board of Veterans' Appeals. Most veterans benefit from a VSO before appealing.
