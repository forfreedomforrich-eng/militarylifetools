How BAH Works in 2026

BAH (Basic Allowance for Housing) is a monthly allowance paid to active-duty service members who don't live in government-provided housing on base. It's designed to cover the cost of off-base housing in your local duty station area.

The 4 Factors That Determine Your BAH

  1. Duty Station ZIP Code (MHA) — The single biggest factor. An E-5 in San Diego gets 3x what an E-5 in rural Missouri gets.
  2. Pay Grade (Rank) — Officers and senior enlisted receive more. E-1 to E-5 share a tier, E-6 to E-9 another, O-1 to O-3 a higher tier, etc.
  3. Dependency Status — "With dependents" rates are 10-30% higher than "without dependents" rates.
  4. Whether You Live On or Off Base — On-base housing forfeits BAH (you get the quarters in-kind).

BAH vs. Old "BAQ" System

If you served before 2008, you may remember the old Basic Allowance for Quarters (BAQ) and Variable Housing Allowance (VHA) system. They were merged into the single BAH in 2008 to simplify the calculation. The modern BAH formula:

BAH Formula (Simplified)

BAH = (Median Rent in MHA for Comparable Housing + Average Utilities) × Rank Adjustment Factor

The DoD sets rates to cover approximately 95% of estimated housing costs. Members cover the remaining 5%+ out of pocket.

2026 BAH Changes & Updates

The 2026 BAH rates include several major changes worth knowing about:

1. 5.2% Average Increase

For 2026, the DoD approved a 5.2% average BAH increase across all MHAs. This is the largest BAH jump since 2022 and is designed to keep pace with rental inflation in high-cost markets. Some MHAs saw increases of 7-9% (San Diego, Honolulu, Seattle), while others saw 2-3% (mid-cost Southern bases).

2. New MHAs Created

The DoD added 3 new Military Housing Areas in 2026 to better reflect local rental markets:

  • Fort Johnson (formerly Fort Polk), LA — separated from the Leesville MHA
  • NAS Whidbey Island, WA — separated from the Oak Harbor MHA
  • Camp Humphreys, South Korea — OHA (Overseas Housing Allowance) recalibration

3. BAH-Type II Phased Out

BAH-Type II (partial BAH paid to some members living on base in specific circumstances) was fully phased out in 2025. Members in this category now receive either full BAH or no BAH based on their specific orders.

4. Inflation Protection for High-Cost Areas

2026 rates include a new "High-Cost Area Stabilization" factor that prevents BAH from dropping more than 10% year-over-year in markets with volatile rents (San Francisco, Seattle, Boston). This protects members from sudden BAH cuts in markets where rents spike.

BAH Strategy: Live On Base vs. Off Base

One of the most common financial decisions service members face is whether to live on base or off base. Here's how to think about it:

When to Live On Base

  • High-cost areas (San Diego, DC, Honolulu, NY) — On-base housing is often a better deal than renting in these markets
  • Junior enlisted (E-1 to E-4) — BAH often doesn't fully cover high rents; on-base housing is guaranteed and predictable
  • Families with kids — On-base schools, BX, commissary, and youth programs save thousands annually
  • Short tours (1-2 years) — Avoids breaking a lease and moving costs

When to Live Off Base

  • Mid- to senior-rank (E-6 and above) — BAH usually exceeds local rent, so you can save/invest the difference
  • Low-cost areas (Texas, Oklahoma, the South) — Off-base BAH often covers rent plus utilities with money left over
  • Buying a home — Use the VA loan (zero down, no PMI) to buy a home and let BAH pay the mortgage
  • Long tours (3+ years) — More time to recoup moving costs and benefit from equity

💡 Pro Tip: The "BAH Arbitrage" Strategy

Many savvy service members practice "BAH Arbitrage" — renting a home for less than their BAH rate and pocketing the difference. Example: An E-6 at Fort Cavazos might receive $2,400/month in BAH but rent a nice 3-bedroom house for $1,500, putting $900/month into savings or a TSP contribution. Over a 4-year tour, that's $43,200 saved.

The VA Loan + BAH Strategy

Another powerful strategy is combining your VA loan benefit with BAH:

  1. Use your VA loan (0% down, no PMI) to buy a home at your duty station
  2. Use BAH to pay the mortgage
  3. After 2+ years, when you PCS, rent out the home using a property manager
  4. Build a real estate portfolio over a 20-year career

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is BAH for E-5 with dependents in 2026?

BAH for E-5 with dependents in 2026 ranges from approximately $1,560/month at low-cost duty stations like Fort Leonard Wood to $4,200+/month at high-cost areas like San Diego, Honolulu, and the San Francisco Bay Area. The national average is around $2,400/month. Rates depend on your duty station ZIP code, rank, and dependency status.

<

When are 2026 BAH rates released?

2026 BAH rates were released by the Department of Defense on January 1, 2026, and took effect on January 1, 2026. The rates are typically updated annually based on local rental market data collected by the DoD.

<

Do I get BAH if I live in government housing on base?

No, you do not receive BAH if you live in on-base government quarters (with some exceptions for partial BAH). When you live on base, your housing is provided in-kind, so you forfeit the BAH allowance. Some service members on orders less than 30 days may receive partial BAH, and those in unique situations (e.g., housing shortage) may receive BAH-Type I or BAH-Type II.

<

Is BAH taxable in 2026?

No, BAH is tax-free at the federal level, and in most states it's also exempt from state income tax. States like California, New York, and others that follow federal tax treatment do not tax BAH. However, you should consult a tax professional for state-specific rules, especially if you maintain a State of Legal Residence (SLR) in a different state.

<

Can I use BAH to pay off my mortgage if I live in a different ZIP code?

Generally, you must live within the local commuting area of your duty station to receive full BAH. If you own a home elsewhere and rent near your duty station, you can use BAH for either. Some exceptions exist for separated members, divorce situations, and members stationed in areas with inadequate housing. Always check with your local finance office for your specific situation.

<

How is BAH calculated for 2026?

BAH is calculated based on (1) local median rental costs for your duty station ZIP code area, (2) average utility costs, (3) your rank, and (4) your dependency status. The DoD surveys rental markets annually and sets rates to cover approximately 95% of housing costs for service members in each Military Housing Area (MHA). 2026 rates reflect a 5.2% average increase aligned with the 2026 military pay raise.

<

What is the difference between BAH and MHA?

MHA stands for Military Housing Area, which is the geographic region used to calculate BAH rates. Each duty station is assigned to an MHA based on local rental market conditions. MHAs are typically defined by ZIP code clusters around major bases. If you live outside your assigned MHA, you may receive a different (usually lower) BAH rate.
