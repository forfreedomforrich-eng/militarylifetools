VA Disability Compensation Explained: What Veterans Should Know in 2026

Practical guide · Updated 2026 · 10 min read

VA disability compensation is one of the most important benefits available to veterans, but it is also one of the most misunderstood. Many veterans want to understand how the system works, what it pays, and whether they should file a claim or appeal an existing decision.

The good news is that the basics are not as complicated as they first appear. At a high level, VA disability compensation is tax-free financial support for veterans with service-connected conditions. If a condition is linked to military service, the VA may assign a percentage-based disability rating, which can affect the amount of monthly compensation a veteran receives.

How VA ratings work

One reason the topic feels confusing is that the VA does not simply add every condition together as a straight number. Instead, it uses a combined ratings system that considers how each condition affects a veteran's overall functioning. The math is similar to a sequence of insurance deductions: 50% and 30% combine to about 65%, not 80%.

Common conditions that lead to claims

Some of the most common conditions connected to VA disability claims include PTSD, tinnitus, hearing loss, migraines, back pain, knee injuries, sleep apnea, and anxiety or depression. These conditions are common because they can stem from deployments, training, injuries, or long-term stress during service.

What veterans should keep in mind

If you are considering filing a claim, the most important step is to collect strong documentation. That may include service records, medical records, private physician statements, and a clear explanation of how the condition affects work, sleep, relationships, and daily functioning. You will also need a copy of your DD-214 to prove service and, for claims after 2014, most service treatment records.

Final thoughts

VA disability compensation is a major benefit, but the process can be difficult to navigate without context. The best approach is to start with a simple overview, use a calculator for planning, and gather the strongest evidence possible before moving forward.

Frequently asked questions

What is VA disability compensation?

VA disability compensation is a tax-free monthly payment to veterans with service-connected conditions. The VA assigns a percentage rating (0% to 100%) to each condition, combines them using a special formula, and pays a monthly amount based on the combined rating and dependents.

How is VA disability rating calculated?

The VA does not simply add ratings. Instead, it uses 'combined ratings' with a formula that treats each rating as a remaining-ability factor. For example, 50% and 30% combine to about 65%, not 80%. The combined rating is then rounded to the nearest 10.

What is the 2026 VA disability pay rate?

For 2026, a single veteran with no dependents at 10% receives about $175/month, while 100% receives about $3,946/month (not including special monthly compensation). Each dependent adds a small amount. Rates are adjusted annually for cost-of-living.

How long does a VA claim take?

As of 2026, average VA claim processing time is around 4-6 months for original claims and 3-5 months for supplemental claims. Fully Developed Claims (FDCs) submitted with all evidence upfront are typically faster than standard claims.

Can I work with VA disability?

Yes. Veterans with any VA disability rating can work. Those rated at 100% (schedular or TDIU) can use the Individual Unemployability benefit if their service-connected conditions prevent them from maintaining substantially gainful employment.

What is a VA disability combined rating?

A combined rating is the VA's blended rating across multiple conditions. It uses the formula 100 - (100 - R1) * (100 - R2) * ..., where R1 and R2 are individual ratings. The result is rounded to the nearest 10. Use our VA disability calculator to estimate your combined rating.
