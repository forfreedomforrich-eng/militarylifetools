TITLE: Military Leave Calculator 2026: Estimate Your Balance, Plan Terminal Leave, and Avoid Surprises at ETS

Republished from the Military Life Tools resource library for WordPress Reader followers who want a structured overview of military leave accrual, balance tracking, and ETS planning.

Military leave is one of the most valuable benefits available to active-duty service members, but it is also one of the most underused. A leave calculator helps service members estimate their balance, plan vacations, and avoid surprises at separation.

HOW MILITARY LEAVE ACCRUES

Active-duty service members earn 2.5 days of leave per month, which works out to 30 days per year. Accrual begins on the first day of service and continues every month as long as the service member remains on active duty.

Unlike civilian leave policies, military leave has no annual cap on carryover. Service members can accumulate a large balance if they rarely take leave, which can become a meaningful asset at separation if planned correctly.

THE MILITARY LEAVE YEAR

The military leave year runs from October 1 to September 30. This is different from the calendar year and the federal fiscal year, and it is one of the most common sources of confusion for service members trying to track their balance.

For leave planning purposes, it is helpful to think in terms of leave years rather than calendar years. A service member who joined in March and wants to plan their leave through the following December should consider both the current leave year and the next one when estimating their balance.

THE 60-DAY WINDOW AT SEPARATION

When a service member separates from active duty, they have a 60-day window in which any unused leave must be used or sold back. After the 60-day window closes, the leave is lost.

This is the most important reason to plan leave carefully in the months leading up to ETS. A service member who knows their projected balance can decide whether to use the leave as terminal leave, sell it back, or split the difference.

TERMINAL LEAVE VS SELL BACK

Terminal leave means taking the leave as time off between the service member's last day of work and their official separation date. Many service members use terminal leave to transition into civilian life, start school, or take a long-overdue vacation.

Sell back means converting the leave balance to cash at the basic pay rate in effect on the date of separation. This is paid out as a lump sum on the final LES.

Both options have value, and many service members split their balance between the two. A leave calculator helps with this decision by providing a clear estimate of how much leave will be available.

WHAT A LEAVE CALCULATOR DOES

A leave calculator typically takes four inputs: current leave balance, leave used so far this year, the date or months remaining until ETS, and sometimes the service member's component or branch. The calculator then estimates the projected leave balance at ETS.

The result is an estimate, not an official figure. The official balance should always be verified through the service member's unit, the personnel system, or the LES. That said, an estimate is a useful starting point for planning.
