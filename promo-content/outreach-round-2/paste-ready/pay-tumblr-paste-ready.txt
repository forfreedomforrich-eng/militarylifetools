TITLE: 2026 Military Pay Raise: 3.8% Is the Headline, But BAH Is Where the Real Money Is

Quick pay-comparison explainer for Tumblr — easy to share, easy to reblog, useful for service members trying to understand their full 2026 compensation.

The 2026 military pay raise is 3.8 percent. That is the largest pay raise in more than 20 years, and it applies to base pay across every rank and years-of-service bracket.

But base pay is only one of three main components of military compensation. The other two are BAH and BAS, and they often make a much bigger difference in a service member's monthly take-home than the base pay raise does.

Here is the simple breakdown:

- Base pay: Determined by rank and years of service. Taxable. Increased 3.8% in 2026.
- BAH (Basic Allowance for Housing): Determined by duty station ZIP code, rank, and dependency status. Tax-free. Average increase ~5.2% in 2026.
- BAS (Basic Allowance for Subsistence): Flat monthly amount for food. Tax-free. ~$315/month for enlisted, ~$260/month for officers.

The most common mistake service members make is comparing military base pay to a civilian salary. That comparison almost always makes military pay look worse than it really is, because it ignores BAH, BAS, healthcare, the GI Bill, and the retirement pension.

A military pay calculator fixes that. Enter your rank, years of service, and duty station ZIP code, and you get a full breakdown of base pay + BAH + BAS, with a total monthly and annual compensation number that can be compared directly to a civilian salary.

For service members in high-cost areas, BAH often makes up 40 percent or more of total compensation. For service members in lower-cost areas, the share is smaller, but still meaningful.
