TITLE: How Much BAH Will You Get in 2026? It Depends on Your ZIP Code More Than Your Rank

Quick BAH explainer for Tumblr — shareable, reblog-friendly, useful for service members and military spouses trying to figure out their 2026 housing allowance.

Most service members assume BAH is mostly about rank. It is not. Your duty station ZIP code is the single biggest factor in how much housing allowance you receive, and it can swing your monthly BAH by thousands of dollars at the same rank.

Three things drive your BAH:

1. Your duty station ZIP code (MHA) — the biggest factor by far
2. Your pay grade — matters, but usually smaller than people think
3. Your dependency status — with dependents is usually 10 to 30 percent higher

For 2026, the national average BAH increase is around 5.2 percent. That is one of the largest jumps in recent years. But the national average is just an average. Some duty stations will see much bigger increases, while others will see smaller changes or even small drops.

Examples for E-5 with dependents in 2026:

- San Diego, CA (Naval Base San Diego, 92135): about $4,212/month
- Pearl Harbor, HI (JB Pearl Harbor-Hickam, 96818): about $4,392/month
- Fort Leonard Wood, MO: about $1,560/month
- Fort Cavazos, TX (76544): about $1,968/month
- JB Anacostia-Bolling, DC (20301): about $3,612/month

Same rank. Same dependency status. Wildly different monthly allowance, just because of where you are stationed.

Once you start receiving BAH at a duty station, you are usually protected against future rate decreases. This is called individual rate protection. But rate protection does not transfer to a new duty station. When you PCS, your BAH resets to the current published rate for the new location.
