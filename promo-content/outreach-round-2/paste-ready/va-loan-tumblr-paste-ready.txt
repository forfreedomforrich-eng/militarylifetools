TITLE: The VA Loan Mistake That Leaves Borrowers House-Poor

Quick VA loan explainer for Tumblr — shareable, reblog-friendly, useful for service members and veterans thinking about buying a home.

The VA loan is one of the best benefits available to veterans and active-duty service members. Zero down payment. No PMI. Competitive rates. For most first-time buyers, it is the only realistic path to homeownership.

But the most common VA loan mistake is treating "no down payment" as "no limit."

THE DEBT-TO-INCOME RULE

Lenders do not approve VA loans based on price alone. They look at your debt-to-income ratio (DTI), which is the sum of all your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income.

Most lenders apply a DTI guideline of around 41 percent. That means your mortgage payment (including taxes and insurance) plus your other monthly debts should not exceed 41 percent of your gross monthly income.

If you make $6,000/month gross and your monthly debts (excluding the new mortgage) total $1,500, your maximum mortgage payment is roughly $960/month. That translates to a specific home price based on the current interest rate, taxes, and insurance — not a vague "you qualify for $500,000."

WHY AFFORDABILITY MATTERS MORE THAN THE LOAN AMOUNT

There is no fixed VA loan limit for most borrowers in 2026. That sounds great, but it actually means lenders will happily approve you for more than you can comfortably afford.

A VA loan affordability calculator fixes this. It applies the 41 percent DTI rule to your income and debts, then returns a comfortable home price and a realistic monthly payment. The result is what you can afford — not what you can technically borrow.

The best approach: figure out what monthly payment you can afford, then look at homes in that price range. Skip the "max loan amount" math entirely.
