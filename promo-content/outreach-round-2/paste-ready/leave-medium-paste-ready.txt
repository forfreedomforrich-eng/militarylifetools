TITLE: Military Leave in 2026: How to Know How Much You Have, and How to Use It Before You Lose It
SUBTITLE: A practical guide to military leave accrual, leave balance tracking, and how to plan terminal leave before ETS.

For Medium's military community — a plain-language walkthrough of how military leave works, why most service members either underuse or miscount their leave, and how a simple calculator can save thousands of dollars at separation.

If you have ever sat down at the end of a long year and wondered how much leave you actually have, you are not alone. Most service members do not track their leave carefully, and most do not understand how quickly the balance can either grow or disappear depending on how they plan.

Military leave is generous, but only if you actually use it. If you let it pile up year after year and then leave the military without a plan, you risk losing a meaningful amount of paid time off that you have already earned.

HOW MILITARY LEAVE ACCRUES

Active-duty service members earn 2.5 days of leave per month, which works out to 30 days per year. That is a significant benefit. By comparison, the average civilian worker in the United States receives about 11 days of paid vacation per year for the first five years on the job.

Leave accrues every month and continues to accrue as long as the service member remains on active duty. There is no cap on how much leave can be carried over from one year to the next, which means service members who rarely take leave can build up balances of 60, 90, or even 120 days over a career.

THE 60-DAY WINDOW AT SEPARATION

Here is where many service members get surprised. When a service member separates from active duty, they have a 60-day window in which any unused leave must be used or sold back. After that 60-day window closes, the leave is lost.

Selling back leave is not a bad outcome. The service member receives basic pay for the leave balance at separation. But many service members prefer to use the leave as terminal leave, which means taking the time off between their last day of work and their official separation date. Terminal leave gives the service member a few extra weeks to transition, travel, or simply decompress before the next chapter.

A leave calculator helps with both options. It tells you how much leave you should have at a given future date, which makes it easier to plan terminal leave, sell back leave, or mix the two.

WHY PEOPLE MISCOUNT THEIR LEAVE

The most common reason service members miscount their leave is that they rely on memory rather than their Leave and Earnings Statement. Accrual happens automatically every month, and unless the service member is checking their LES regularly, it is easy to forget exactly how much they have.

Another reason is confusion around the leave year. The military leave year runs from October 1 to September 30, which does not line up with the calendar year or the fiscal year. Service members who plan around the calendar year may misjudge how much leave they have used and how much they have remaining.

A leave calculator helps clear up that confusion by working in clear, simple inputs and producing an estimate that the service member can sanity-check against their LES.

HOW TO USE A LEAVE CALCULATOR

The best leave calculators ask for a small number of inputs: current balance, leave used so far this year, and either the ETS date or the number of months remaining. From there, the calculator estimates how much leave the service member will have at a given future point.

That kind of estimate is useful for planning a vacation, scheduling terminal leave, or simply confirming that the official balance matches what the service member expects.
