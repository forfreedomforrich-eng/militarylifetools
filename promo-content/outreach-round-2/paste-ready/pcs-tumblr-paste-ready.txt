TITLE: The #1 Mistake Service Members Make During a PCS (And How to Avoid It)

Quick PCS-planning explainer for Tumblr — shareable, reblog-friendly, useful for service members who want to make their next move less painful.

The biggest PCS mistake is starting too late.

Most service members try to plan their entire move in the last two weeks. By then, the household goods shipping office is slammed, temporary lodging rates are at their highest, and the best housing at the new duty station is already taken.

A better approach is to start 8 to 12 weeks before the move. That gives you time to:

- Schedule household goods shipment at a reasonable rate
- Search for housing at the new duty station (and compare options, not just take the first one you see)
- Coordinate school transitions for kids
- Give proper notice on your current lease
- Book temporary lodging in advance

The pre-move phase is where most of the high-stakes decisions happen. Move week is mostly logistics. Post-arrival is mostly administrative cleanup.

A timeline-based PCS checklist helps by organizing every task by when it needs to happen, not just what it is. That way, nothing falls through the cracks, and you arrive at your new duty station with a clear head instead of a stack of unsolved problems.
