TITLE: BAH Calculator 2026: Find Your Exact Housing Allowance by ZIP Code, Rank, and Dependency Status

Long-form BAH reference for Blogger readers who want the full picture of how the 2026 housing allowance is calculated and what to expect in their area.

The Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH) is one of the most important parts of military compensation, but it is also one of the most confusing. Rates change every year, they depend heavily on duty station ZIP code, and they vary by rank and dependency status. Many service members and their families want to understand exactly how much they will receive in 2026 before they sign a lease or plan a move.

A reliable BAH calculator helps cut through the confusion. Instead of guessing or relying on outdated tables, a service member can enter their duty station ZIP code, pay grade, and dependency status and get a realistic monthly estimate within seconds. That estimate is useful for budgeting, planning a PCS, or comparing offers between duty stations.

HOW BAH IS CALCULATED IN 2026

The Department of Defense calculates BAH using three main factors: the duty station ZIP code (also called the Military Housing Area or MHA), the service member's pay grade, and whether they have dependents. Each MHA has its own local rate based on housing cost data collected in that area.

For 2026, the average BAH increase is approximately 5.2 percent over 2025 rates. That is one of the largest increases in recent years and reflects continued growth in rental and housing costs around many military installations.

Pay grade matters because senior enlisted members and officers typically receive higher BAH. For example, an E-9 with dependents will receive more than an E-3 with dependents at the same duty station. Dependency status matters as well. Service members with dependents usually receive a rate that is roughly 10 to 30 percent higher than single members at the same rank and location.

WHY THE DUTY STATION ZIP CODE MATTERS MOST

The duty station ZIP code is the single biggest factor in BAH. Two service members at the same rank with the same dependency status can receive dramatically different allowances depending only on where they are stationed.

For instance, an E-5 with dependents stationed in San Diego may receive more than $4,200 per month, while the same service member stationed at Fort Leonard Wood may receive closer to $1,560 per month. That is a difference of thousands of dollars per month and tens of thousands over a full tour.

This is why service members planning a PCS often look carefully at BAH differences between locations. The allowance can make one duty station significantly more attractive than another, especially for families trying to stretch their budget or save money.

RATE PROTECTION AND WHY IT MATTERS

Once a service member starts receiving BAH at a duty station, they generally receive rate protection. This means that if the BAH rate for that location decreases in a future year, the service member will continue to receive the higher rate they were originally entitled to as long as they stay at the same duty station without a break in eligibility.

Rate protection does not, however, transfer to a new duty station. When a service member PCSes, their BAH resets to the current published rate for the new location, rank, and dependency status. If that new rate is lower, the service member begins receiving the lower amount right away.

Understanding rate protection can help a service member decide whether to stay at a current duty station longer, accept a new assignment, or plan around an upcoming move.

A PRACTICAL WAY TO USE A BAH CALCULATOR

The easiest way to use a BAH calculator is to enter a duty station ZIP code, pay grade, and dependency status, and then compare the result with the actual housing costs in that area. A calculator is most useful when paired with a quick check of local rent prices.

For service members who can find housing below their BAH rate, the difference is kept tax-free. That makes BAH one of the most flexible parts of military compensation, because the money saved by renting wisely can be redirected to savings, TSP contributions, or other goals.
