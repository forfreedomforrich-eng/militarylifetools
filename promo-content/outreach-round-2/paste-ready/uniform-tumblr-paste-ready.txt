TITLE: How to Measure Yourself for a Military Uniform (The 4 Numbers You Need)

Quick uniform-sizing explainer for Tumblr — shareable, reblog-friendly, useful for service members ordering uniforms online or between fittings.

Most service members get measured once when they join, then guess forever after. That usually works until it does not, and then you are standing in a uniform shop wondering why your new uniform fits weird.

Here is how to measure yourself for a military uniform, in four steps:

1. Chest — Wrap the measuring tape around the fullest part of your chest, under your armpits. Keep it horizontal and snug but not tight.

2. Waist — Measure around your natural waistline (just above the belly button). Do not suck in.

3. Height — Stand against a wall with heels together. Have someone else measure from the floor to the top of your head.

4. Inseam — From your crotch to the bottom of your ankle along the inside of your leg.

THOSE FOUR NUMBERS ARE ENOUGH

With those four measurements, you can find your size across Army, Navy, Air Force, and Marine Corps uniform standards, and convert between US, EU, and UK sizing systems.

ONE RULE: SIZE UP IF YOU ARE BETWEEN SIZES

Military uniforms are designed to be worn tucked in. A slightly larger size is easier to tailor, more comfortable in the field, and more forgiving when your weight changes over a career. Sizing down rarely works out.
