Every year the Department of Defense releases updated BAH tables, and every year service members across every branch have the same questions. How much will my BAH be this year? Why is my BAH so different from the national average? Why did my BAH go up less than my buddy's? A reliable BAH calculator helps answer those questions in seconds, but it helps even more when the service member understands the factors driving the calculation.

WHAT IS BAH?

BAH stands for Basic Allowance for Housing. It is a monthly allowance paid to active-duty service members who do not live in government-provided housing on base. The purpose of BAH is to help offset the cost of off-base housing in the local civilian market.

BAH is not taxable at the federal level, and in most states it is also exempt from state income tax. That makes it one of the most valuable components of military compensation, because the entire amount goes directly toward housing or savings.

THE THREE FACTORS THAT DRIVE BAH

1. Duty Station ZIP Code (MHA) — the most important factor. Each Military Housing Area has its own published rate based on local rental cost data.
2. Pay Grade — officers and senior enlisted members receive higher rates than junior enlisted members at the same duty station.
3. Dependency Status — service members with dependents typically receive a higher rate than single members, usually in the 10 to 30 percent range.

Of these three, the duty station ZIP code usually has the largest impact. Two service members at the same rank with the same dependency status can see BAH differences of $2,000 or more per month depending only on where they are stationed.

2026 BAH INCREASES

For 2026, the average BAH increase is approximately 5.2 percent over 2025 rates. That is one of the largest increases in the past several years and reflects continued growth in rental and housing costs around many military installations.

It is important to note, however, that the 5.2 percent is a national average. Some MHAs will see larger increases, while others may see smaller increases or even small decreases. The only way to know your specific number is to look up your duty station ZIP code in the published tables or in a BAH calculator.

RATE PROTECTION EXPLAINED

Once a service member begins receiving BAH at a duty station, they are generally protected against future rate decreases. If the BAH rate for that MHA decreases in a future year, the service member will continue to receive the higher rate they were originally entitled to, as long as they stay at the same duty station without a break in eligibility.

Rate protection does not transfer to a new duty station. When a service member PCSes, their BAH resets to the current published rate for the new location. That is one reason service members planning a PCS often compare BAH between potential new duty stations in advance.

HOW A BAH CALCULATOR HELPS

A BAH calculator takes the three inputs (ZIP code, pay grade, dependency status) and returns an estimated monthly allowance based on the most recent published tables. The best calculators are fast, accurate, and built around the current year's rates.

For service members who want to budget, plan a move, or compare two duty stations, a BAH calculator is often the simplest way to get a quick estimate without having to navigate the full DoD BAH lookup tool.
