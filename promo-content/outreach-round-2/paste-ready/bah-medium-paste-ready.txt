TITLE: BAH in 2026: What Every Service Member Should Know Before They Sign a Lease
SUBTITLE: A clear-eyed look at how the 2026 housing allowance is calculated, why your ZIP code matters more than your rank, and how to make BAH work for you.

For Medium's military and veterans community — a plain-language guide to Basic Allowance for Housing in 2026, including the biggest mistake service members make when planning around their allowance.

Every year, a few weeks after the new BAH tables drop, the same questions start showing up across military Facebook groups, Reddit threads, and base housing offices. Why is my BAH so different from my friend's? Why did my neighbor's BAH go up while mine stayed the same? And the most common one of all: how do I know I am actually getting the right amount?

The honest answer is that BAH is simpler than people think, but it is also more location-sensitive than most service members realize. Once you understand the three factors that drive the calculation, the whole thing becomes much easier to navigate.

THE THREE FACTORS THAT DRIVE BAH

BAH is calculated using three inputs: your duty station ZIP code, your pay grade, and your dependency status. That is it. The Department of Defense publishes a table for every Military Housing Area (MHA), and the table tells the system exactly what to pay based on those three inputs.

Of those three, the duty station ZIP code is by far the most important. An E-5 with dependents in San Diego can receive more than $4,200 per month, while the same E-5 with dependents at Fort Leonard Wood receives closer to $1,560. The pay grade matters, but the ZIP code usually dominates the final number.

Dependency status matters too, but its impact is smaller. Moving from "without dependents" to "with dependents" usually bumps BAH by 10 to 30 percent depending on the MHA. For most families, the bigger lever is location, not rank.

WHY BAH IS SO LOCATION-SENSITIVE

The DoD updates BAH tables every year using local rental cost data. If rents in a particular MHA have gone up faster than the national average, the BAH rate for that area will go up faster too. If rents have stayed flat or dropped, the rate may stay flat or even decrease.

For 2026, the average BAH increase is about 5.2 percent, which is one of the largest jumps in recent years. But that is just the national average. Some MHAs will see much larger increases, while others may see smaller changes or even slight decreases. The only way to know your specific number is to look up your duty station ZIP code, not the national average.

This is the most common mistake service members make. They hear about a national BAH increase and assume they will receive the full bump. In reality, your personal increase depends on what happened in your specific housing market.

RATE PROTECTION EXPLAINED

If you are already at a duty station and your BAH decreases in a future year, you may still receive the higher rate you were originally entitled to. This is called individual rate protection, and it applies as long as you maintain uninterrupted eligibility at the same duty station.

Rate protection does not, however, transfer to a new duty station. When you PCS, your BAH resets to whatever the current published rate is for your new location. That is why some service members receive a sudden BAH drop after a move, even if their rank and dependency status have not changed.

Understanding rate protection can help with PCS planning, especially for families comparing the financial impact of different duty stations.

HOW A BAH CALCULATOR FITS IN

A BAH calculator is not an official DoD tool, but it can be a useful planning resource. By entering your ZIP code, pay grade, and dependency status, you can get a quick estimate of what your 2026 housing allowance should look like. That estimate can help you budget, compare duty stations, or sanity-check your LES.

The best calculators are simple, fast, and based on the published 2026 rates. For service members who want to plan around their housing allowance, that kind of tool is often the first step in making a more confident decision about where to live and how to save.
