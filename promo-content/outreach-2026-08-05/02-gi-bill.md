<p>The Post-9/11 GI Bill is one of the most valuable benefits for military members leaving service or planning a career change. The challenge is that the rules are often confusing, especially when housing, tuition caps, and transfer options all come into play.</p>

<h2>What this guide covers</h2>
<ul>
  <li>What percentage of benefits you qualify for</li>
  <li>How much tuition and housing the GI Bill may cover</li>
  <li>Whether the Yellow Ribbon Program helps at private schools</li>
  <li>How to estimate value before applying</li>
</ul>

<h2>How the benefit works</h2>
<p>Your GI Bill tier depends on your total post-9/11 active-duty service. A longer service record usually means a higher eligibility percentage (40%, 60%, 80%, or 100%). Many veterans compare school tuition and housing costs against their expected benefit amount before making a decision.</p>

<h2>Tuition vs. housing vs. books</h2>
<p>The Post-9/11 GI Bill typically covers full tuition at in-state public schools, a monthly housing allowance based on the school's ZIP code, and an annual stipend for books and supplies. Private and out-of-state schools often require Yellow Ribbon or out-of-pocket tuition.</p>
